# Predictive Modeling for Horse Racing: What the Literature and Practitioner Evidence Actually Say

*Research audit for an Ireland-first (UK-relevant) bet-suggestion app. Compiled 2026-08-28. Priority given to primary sources (full texts of Benter 1994, Bolton & Chapman 1986, and Lessmann/Sung/Johnson 2007 were extracted and read in full).*

## Key takeaways

- **The problem is solved in principle and the blueprint is public.** Bolton & Chapman (1986) established the multinomial/conditional logit framework; Bill Benter's 1994 paper describes, step by step, the architecture that made his Hong Kong syndicate roughly USD $1B: a fundamental model whose log-probabilities are **combined with the public's log-odds probabilities in a second-stage logit**. That two-stage "model + market" design is the single most load-bearing idea in the entire literature ([Benter 1994](https://gwern.net/doc/statistics/decision/1994-benter.pdf), [Bloomberg 2018](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/features/2018-05-03/the-gambler-who-cracked-the-horse-racing-code)).
- **A standalone model is worthless without the market.** Benter showed that his fundamental model's probabilities were systematically biased toward its own opinion; horses it liked more than the public won far less often than it predicted (his Table 4). Only after blending with the odds were probabilities unbiased and usable for staking. Benter called incorporating public odds "his single most important innovation" ([Bloomberg 2018](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/features/2018-05-03/the-gambler-who-cracked-the-horse-racing-code)).
- **The measurable target is tiny.** Benter's combined-model pseudo-R² was 0.1396 vs 0.1218 for the public odds alone — an information gain (ΔR²) of just 0.0178 over 3,198 races — and that was enough for large profits. Conversely, a 48-tipster consensus with a standalone R² almost equal to his fundamental model (0.1014 vs 0.1016) added *nothing* once combined with the odds (ΔR² = 0.0002). The test of any feature/model is **incremental information over the odds**, not raw accuracy ([Benter 1994](https://gwern.net/doc/statistics/decision/1994-benter.pdf)).
- **Modern ML replications on UK/HK data support gradient boosting / kernel methods over plain conditional logit, with the same two-stage design.** Lessmann, Sung & Johnson (Journal of Prediction Markets, 2007) got +17.1% ROI on a 156-race UK (Goodwood) holdout with an SVR+conditional-logit two-stage model vs +1.75% for pure conditional logit; their 2010 International Journal of Forecasting paper found random forests beat CL/SVM on 1,000 HK races (12,902 runners). All published ROI figures come from small holdouts and should be treated as proof-of-concept, not expected returns ([JPM PDF](https://www.ubplj.org/index.php/jpm/article/download/427/459/1346), [IJF 2010](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0169207009002143)).
- **Ireland just became modellable at a deeper level:** sectional timing (Coursetrack) was rolled out at **all 26 Irish tracks from 1 January 2024**, with data published for every race — a feature the market has had only ~2.5 years to absorb, i.e. a plausible edge area ([Racing Post](https://www.racingpost.com/news/ireland/long-awaited-sectional-timing-data-for-irish-racecourses-to-be-rolled-out-at-all-26-irish-tracks-from-january-1-ax22E4d35aLd/)).
- **Staking is as important as prediction:** use fractional Kelly (½ or ⅓). Benter warns that overestimating edge by 2x turns full Kelly into negative growth; half-Kelly gives ~75% of the growth at ~half the volatility (MacLean/Ziemba/Blazenko 1992). Evaluate the model on **closing line value (beating Betfair SP)**, not short-term P&L.
- **Realistic edges are small and capacity-limited.** Benter estimated max extractable profit at 0.25–0.5% of total race turnover (hard ceiling ~1.5%) even with an infinite bankroll; Betfair's Premium Charge (up to 40–60% of gross profits, levied on <0.5% of customers) is systemic proof that persistent winners exist *and* that the platform taxes them ([Betfair](https://support.betfair.com/app/answers/detail/betfair-charges/)).

## 1. The canonical work

### Bolton & Chapman (1986) — the founding paper

"Searching for Positive Returns at the Track: A Multinomial Logit Model for Handicapping Horse Races," *Management Science* 32(8), 1040–1060 ([INFORMS](https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/10.1287/mnsc.32.8.1040), [full PDF](https://gwern.net/doc/statistics/decision/1986-bolton.pdf)). Read in full for this audit. Key contents:

- **Model:** multinomial logit over the runners in each race — each horse gets a utility score from a linear combination of features; softmax over the race gives win probabilities that sum to 1. This is exactly today's "conditional logit" and the ancestor of modern listwise/softmax ranking losses.
- **Data:** only **200 US races** (Aqueduct 43, Pimlico 52, Garden State 42, Keystone 32, Suffolk Downs 31), hand-coded from the Daily Racing Form at ~1 hour per race. They used "explosion" (rank-ordered logit, depth 3) to squeeze ~3x effective sample from the finish order — the same trick as training on full finishing ranks rather than win/lose.
- **10 features:** `LIFE%WIN` (career win %), `AVESPRAT` (avg speed rating last 4 runs), `W/RACE` (earnings per race), `LSPEDRAT` (last-race speed rating), `JOCK%WIN`, `JOCK#WIN`, `JMISDATA` (missing-jockey-data flag), `WEIGHT`, `POSTPOS` (draw), `NEWDIST` (distance-switch flag).
- **Wagering results (4×50-race holdouts):** naive expected-value betting (Isaacs) lost −27.8%; Kelly-style (Rosner) lost −6.4% to −37%; **only after adding a side constraint banning bets on longshots (model probability < ~0.07–0.11) did returns go positive**. Their diagnosis — relative probability errors are largest on longshots — remains the classic justification for minimum-probability / minimum-edge filters.

### Benter (1994) — the practitioner blueprint

"Computer Based Horse Race Handicapping and Wagering Systems: A Report," in *Efficiency of Racetrack Betting Markets* ([full PDF](https://gwern.net/doc/statistics/decision/1994-benter.pdf), [World Scientific](https://www.worldscientific.com/doi/abs/10.1142/9789812819192_0019)). Read in full. The most operationally useful paper in the field:

- **Factor groups** (his taxonomy, directly reusable): current condition (recent form, days since last race, workouts, age); past performance (finish positions, lengths beaten, normalised times); adjustments (strength of opposition, weight, jockey, bad luck, draw in past races); today's situational factors (weight, jockey, draw); preferences (distance, surface, going, track).
- **Feature depth matters:** his distance-preference factor `DPGA` regressed the residual of each past run (actual minus predicted finish) on distance similarity, standardised by its standard error — "several thousand lines of code" for one feature. Simplistic factor definitions "do not provide sufficiently accurate estimates."
- **Counterintuitive features are fine if the sample proves them:** "number of past races" was one of his most significant factors with no common-sense story.
- **Minimum data:** 500–1,000 races (with full past-performance data for every runner) for development + testing; gains beyond 1,000 races were modest. He favoured **closed racing populations** (all horses race each other at few tracks — Hong Kong; Ireland is not closed but is small and heavily inter-connected with the UK).
- **The second stage (the crown jewel):** fit `c_i = exp(α·f_i + β·π_i) / Σ_j exp(α·f_j + β·π_j)` where `f` = log of *out-of-sample* fundamental-model probability and `π` = log of public implied probability, α and β by maximum likelihood. On 3,198 HK races (Sept 1986–June 1993; 32,877 runners): public R² 0.1218, fundamental 0.1245, combined 0.1396. His heuristic model-value metric is **ΔR² = R²(combined) − R²(public)**; bigger ΔR² invariably meant more simulated profit.
- **First-timers/unratable horses:** assign them the public probability and renormalise — "we have no information to dispute the public."
- **Exotic bets:** advantage compounds multiplicatively — two horses that are near-value win bets can make a 16% edge quinella. Harville's formula for finish-order probabilities is significantly biased (longshots run 2nd/3rd more than it predicts); he corrects with power-discounts γ=0.81 (2nd), δ=0.65 (3rd), fitted by MLE.
- **Results:** 5 years live, ~470 races bet/year, avg track take ~19%, four of five seasons profitable, worst season −20% of starting capital; ~5 man-years to first profitable model and ~5 more to a highly profitable operation. Pool impact capped profit at **0.25–0.5% of race turnover** (≤1.5% ceiling); HK pools exceeded USD $10M/race.

### The Hong Kong syndicates — Benter, Woods, Ranogajec

- **Bill Benter** and **Alan Woods** formed the first HK computer team in 1984–85, lost $120k of their $150k bankroll in the first (1986) season, split in 1987 after the model returned ~$100k that season. Benter rebuilt alone: ~$600k season circa 1988–89, then — after adding public odds to the model — ~$3M in 1990–91. His model grew from ~20 inputs to **120+ variables per horse**; his operations have made "close to a billion dollars." In 2001 he famously hit a HK$100M+ Triple Trio (staking HK$1.6M across 51,381 combinations) and never claimed the ~$16M prize ([Bloomberg Businessweek, May 2018](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/features/2018-05-03/the-gambler-who-cracked-the-horse-racing-code)).
- **Alan Woods** (1945–2008) ran a rival HK syndicate (with Zeljko Ranogajec involvement in the 1990s via Manila); by 2006–07 it accounted for ~2% of all HKJC turnover; estimated fortune at death AU$670M (Guinness estimate ~A$939M) ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alan_Woods_(gambler))).
- **Zeljko Ranogajec** (b. 1961) scaled the same approach into the world's largest betting operation ("the Bankroll"/Punters Club, ~300 staff, ~A$1B+ annual turnover), reportedly 6–8% of Tabcorp's ~$10B revenue and about a third of Betfair Australia's business; his profitability rests heavily on **negotiated tote rebates** (volume discounts that flip a −15% take into positive EV), which contributed to Tote Tasmania's collapse in 2011. He later co-founded Colossus Bets in the UK ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zeljko_Ranogajec), [iGaming Business](https://igamingbusiness.com/finance/winning-at-wagering/)).
- **Lesson for the app:** the syndicates' edges were (a) model+odds blending, (b) pari-mutuel pools where late money doesn't move your price against you as in fixed-odds, and (c) rebates/scale. A retail Irish/UK bettor gets none of (c), faces bookmaker restrictions on winners, and should therefore target the **Betfair exchange** where winners are tolerated (but taxed via commission and, at scale, the Premium Charge).

## 2. Modern ML literature (2015–2026)

The academic record since Benter is thin but consistent: the conditional-logit skeleton survives; nonlinear learners on top of it help; nothing published credibly obsoletes the two-stage design.

- **Kernel/SVR era (Southampton group).** Lessmann, Sung & Johnson, "Adapting least-square support vector regression models to forecast the outcome of horseraces," *Journal of Prediction Markets* (2007) ([PDF](https://www.ubplj.org/index.php/jpm/article/download/427/459/1346)) — read in full. Data: **556 UK races (Goodwood, May 1995–Aug 2000)**, Bolton–Chapman-style features; 400 train / 156 holdout. Two-stage LS-SVR→CL with Kelly staking returned **+10.96%** (flat) and **+112%** with reinvestment; tuning hyperparameters by NDCG (a ranking metric) instead of MSE raised flat ROI to **+17.08%** (+172% with reinvestment); the two-stage *pure conditional logit* benchmark made only **+1.75%**. Two durable lessons: (1) nonlinearity adds real signal because the public has long since priced the *linear* Bolton–Chapman factors; (2) **optimize ranking of the top of the field, not average error**.
- **Random forests.** Lessmann, Sung & Johnson, "Alternative methods of predicting competitive events," *International Journal of Forecasting* 26(3) 2010, 518–536: adapted random forest vs CL and SVM on **1,000 HK races / 12,902 runners**; RF gave higher accuracy and simulated profitability, again inside a two-stage odds-blending framework ([ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0169207009002143)). Related: Sung & Johnson (2007) showed the two-stage procedure beats one-stage CL — the academic confirmation of Benter's design.
- **Gradient boosting + learning-to-rank (2020s).** A 2025 Korean study compared LightGBM/XGBoost/CatBoost as learning-to-rank models on **9,140 Korea Racing Authority races (May 2024–Apr 2025)**: CatBoost best on ranking quality (NDCG 0.8895), LightGBM/XGBoost better in betting simulations ([KCI](https://journal.kci.go.kr/jksci/archive/articleView?artiId=ART003266151)). A 2024 Mauritius paper applies LTR (pointwise/pairwise/listwise) to Champ de Mars racing ([ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/380208045_Horse_race_rank_prediction_using_learning-to-rank_approaches)). The **Plackett–Luce** model — literally invented for horse-race finish orders — is the theoretical bridge: conditional logit = PL depth 1; "exploded" logit = PL over full ranks; ListNet/ListMLE losses in LightGBM/XGBoost implement it at scale ([Xia et al.](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/335854849_Plackett-Luce_model_for_learning-to-rank_task), [Guiver & Snelson, ICML 2009](https://icml.cc/Conferences/2009/papers/347.pdf)).
- **Deep learning.** Published results are underwhelming relative to GBDTs on tabular racing data: a CUHK final-year thesis used deep probabilistic programming on HK data ([PDF](https://www.cse.cuhk.edu.hk/lyu/_media/thesis/report-1805-1.pdf)); a 2022 arXiv position paper surveys what deep nets could add ([arXiv:2207.04981](https://arxiv.org/abs/2207.04981)); Kaggle notebooks (e.g. [Cullen Sun's HK deep model](https://www.kaggle.com/code/cullensun/deep-learning-model-for-hong-kong-horse-racing)) show plausible classification but no robust ROI. A 2021 Polish study (flat racing, XGBoost/RF/NN/LDA) reports 41% win-bet hit rate ([ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/352778299_MACHINE_LEARNING_IN_THE_PREDICTION_OF_FLAT_HORSE_RACING_RESULTS_IN_POLAND)) — but on small samples and without odds-adjusted evaluation. A 2024 systematic review of ML in sports betting summarises the field's chronic weaknesses: tiny test sets, no CLV benchmarking, leakage ([arXiv:2410.21484](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2410.21484)).
- **Regularised conditional logit** remains competitive: e.g. "Predicting horse race winners through a regularized conditional logistic regression with frailty" ([ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/343894424_PREDICTING_HORSE_RACE_WINNERS_THROUGH_A_REGULARIZED_CONDITIONAL_LOGISTIC_REGRESSION_WITH_FRAILTY)).
- **Market odds: feature vs benchmark.** The consensus procedure, unchanged since Benter: (1) train the fundamental model *without* odds; (2) blend model log-probs with market log-probs in a second-stage logit (this doubles as **probability calibration** — the α, β coefficients shrink the model toward the market exactly as reliability demands); (3) judge the model by ΔR²/log-loss gain over the odds-only model and by simulated flat-Kelly ROI *at exchange prices minus commission*. Putting odds directly into stage 1 as one more feature risks the model collapsing onto the market and leaks nothing to detect. UK market context: the favourite–longshot bias has shrunk over time (average odds error down 13.2% from 1996–2000 to 2001–2005) and is far weaker on exchanges (books ~100.5–101%) than with bookmakers ([Academia](https://www.academia.edu/79313982/Forecasting_horse_race_outcomes_New_evidence_on_odds_bias_in_UK_betting_markets), [arXiv:2402.02623](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2402.02623), [Smarkets](https://help.smarkets.com/hc/en-gb/articles/115000402651-The-effect-of-draw-bias-on-horse-racing-betting)); backing all favourites loses ~5%, all longshots up to ~40% ([sbo.net](https://www.sbo.net/horse-racing/favourite-longshot-bias/)).
- **Credibility of published ROI:** treat every academic ROI claim (+10–17% above; 40%+ hit rates elsewhere) as fragile: holdouts of 150–1,000 races have ROI standard errors of tens of percent (Bolton & Chapman's own four 50-race subsets ranged from −65.9% to +46.4%); many papers use SP rather than achievable exchange prices; almost none model commission, Rule 4, or price impact.

## 3. Feature engineering that matters for UK/Irish racing

- **Official Ratings (OR) and the handicap system.** BHA (and IHRB in Ireland) handicappers publish a rating for every rated horse; **1 rating point = 1 lb of weight**; ratings are re-assessed weekly (BHA updates Tuesdays). Lengths→pounds conversion depends on distance/going: ~3.4 lb/length at 5f good-to-firm down to ~1.2 lb/length at 1m6f ([BHA guide](https://www.britishhorseracing.com/regulation/guide-to-handicapping/), [The Race Lab](https://theracelab.co.uk/guides/how-handicap-ratings-work-in-horse-racing/)). OR is a free, expert-made ability feature; "well-handicapped" detection (horse improving faster than its OR) is a classic edge in the UK/Irish handicap-dominated programme.
- **Speed figures.** Three main commercial families: **Topspeed** (Racing Post) — pure time vs Racing Post standard times, adjusted for going/weight/wind; **RPR** (Racing Post Ratings) — form-based handicapper judgment (100 ≈ horse at 9st on good ground); **Timeform** — form+time hybrid, now incorporating sectionals ([The Race Lab](https://theracelab.co.uk/guides/understanding-speed-figures-in-horse-racing/), [pickawinner](https://pickawinnerhorse.com/articles/horse-racing-speed-figures/), [raceadvisor on Topspeed](https://classic.raceadvisor.co.uk/topspeed-ratings-calculated/)). Building your own figures (time vs per-course standard time, with a daily going allowance solved across the card) replicates Topspeed and is a well-trodden DIY path; note these ratings are *in* the market — the value test is Benter's ΔR², not standalone correlation.
- **Going/ground.** Official going (Heavy→Firm in IRE/UK, plus GoingStick readings in GB) interacts with nearly everything: per-horse going preference (Benter-style residual features), sire going stats, and pace. Irish NH racing is heavily going-sensitive in winter.
- **Draw bias (flat only).** Real, course-specific and largely priced. Chester is the extreme: stalls 1–4 win 61.7% of races since 2010 (stall 1 alone 17.6%) ([flatstats](https://www.flatstats.co.uk/draw-stats.php?course=Chester), [geegeez](https://www.geegeez.co.uk/dave-renham-chester-draw-pace-part-1/)); yet A/E (actual/expected at odds) for low draws at Chester ≈ 0.94, i.e. the market *over*-corrects — a warning that raw win% draw stats without odds adjustment mislead ([onlinebetting.org.uk](https://www.onlinebetting.org.uk/betting-guides/horse-racing/draw-bias-in-horse-racing.html)). Encode as course×distance×going draw-position effects and always evaluate vs odds.
- **Pace / run style.** UK data: front-runners win 14.91% vs prominent 11.42%, midfield 8.11%, held-up 7.25% — front-runners win at twice the rate of hold-up horses, with big course effects (Chester front-runners ~25%; Newmarket July favours closers) ([geegeez pace research via lightspeedstats](https://lightspeedstats.com/horse-racing/pace-and-draw-analysis/), [racingbetter](https://www.racingbetter.co.uk/news/2021/04/rb777_horse_racing_pace.html)). Run-style (EPF) labels are derivable from in-running comments or sectional data; predicted race shape (how many confirmed leaders) is a genuinely under-priced feature per multiple practitioner studies.
- **Trainer/jockey strike rates and form cycles.** Baseline UK trainer strike rate ~10–12%, top yards 15–20%, hot spells 30–40%; 14–28 day form adds signal, and **cold form is more predictive (negatively) than hot form is positively** ([racebrain](https://racebrain.io/blog/trainers-in-form), [fasthorseresultstoday](https://fasthorseresultstoday.com/articles/trainer-statistics/)). Use empirical-Bayes-shrunk trainer/jockey/course/type strike rates, not raw percentages.
- **Days since last run.** US data (BloodHorse/DRF, flag: US dirt racing, not directly transferable): quick returners (1–7 days) win most (ROI −20 to −22%), 181+ day layoffs worst (−32%), win% peaking around 35–40 day gaps, but graded-stakes horses off 181+ days were ROI-positive (+15%) — layoff meaning is class-conditional ([BloodHorse](https://www.bloodhorse.com/horse-racing/articles/292401/odds-and-trends-the-lowdown-on-layoffs)). Model days-since-run as a spline interacted with class and trainer (some Irish yards ready one first time up).
- **Class and weight.** Class moves (official class bands 1–7 in GB; premier/graded structure in IRE) plus weight-adjusted ability (OR vs weight carried) are core; the handicap system makes "weight vs rating differential" and "ahead of the handicapper" features central in a way US literature ignores.
- **Sectional times — the fresh Irish opportunity.** GB: Total Performance Data (GPS) has supplied per-furlong sectionals/stride data for most GB courses for years and sells feeds; Timeform retails GB+IRE sectional data ([Timeform shop](https://www.timeform.com/horse-racing/shop/sectional-times), [TPD blog on At The Races](https://www.attheraces.com/blogs/total-performance-data/08-september-2025/total-performance-data-blog)). Ireland: **Coursetrack sectionals went live at all 26 Irish tracks from 1 Jan 2024** under the HRI/AIR/SIS/RMG media deal, published for every race (day-after on Racing TV results) ([Racing Post](https://www.racingpost.com/news/ireland/long-awaited-sectional-timing-data-for-irish-racecourses-to-be-rolled-out-at-all-26-irish-tracks-from-january-1-ax22E4d35aLd/), [TDN](https://www.thoroughbreddailynews.com/coursetrack-tracking-data-implemented-at-all-irish-racecourses/), [RMG](https://www.racecoursemediagroup.com/news/blog/coursetrack-tracking-data-hailed-by-irish-racecourses-after-successful-roll-out/)). Only ~2.5 seasons of Irish sectional history exist — little for training but the market's pricing of sectional-derived form is immature: a candidate edge.
- **Breeding.** Most useful for lightly-raced horses (2yos, NH bumper/point recruits, first try at a distance/going): sire progeny stats — average winning distance (AWD ≥7.5f = stamina sire, <6.5f = speed sire), sire going splits — act as priors/filters rather than primary features ([TwinSpires/Brisnet](https://www.twinspires.com/edge/racing/using-brisnet-sire-stats-for-pedigree-handicapping/), [geegeez NH sires](https://www.geegeez.co.uk/national-hunt-sire-data-an-edge-part-1/), [drawbias.com sire going stats](https://www.drawbias.com/sirestats.html)). This matters for Ireland where NH recruits debut with zero track form.

## 4. Staking and evaluation

- **Kelly and fractional Kelly.** Kelly (1956) maximises expected log-wealth; single-bet form `K = advantage / (odds − 1)`. Benter's three reasons never to bet full Kelly: (1) if you overestimate your advantage by 2x, full Kelly produces *negative* growth; (2) withdrawals reduce effective wealth; (3) >50% drawdowns are routine at full Kelly. He bet fractional Kelly (e.g. ½ or ⅓) throughout ([Benter 1994](https://gwern.net/doc/statistics/decision/1994-benter.pdf)). MacLean, Ziemba & Blazenko (*Management Science* 1992): half-Kelly ≈ 75% of full-Kelly growth at ~half the volatility ([overview](https://www.worldscientific.com/doi/10.1142/9789811285530_0015), [Ziemba chapter](https://webhomes.maths.ed.ac.uk/mckinnon/blackouts/StochOptFinanceAndEnergySpringer/Chap1_KellyZiemba.pdf)). Practitioner rule of thumb (Betfair Hub): most pros use 20–33% Kelly in racing ([Betfair staking](https://www.betfair.com.au/hub/education/racing-strategy/staking-and-money-management/)). Add Bolton & Chapman's minimum-probability constraint (skip model longshots) and a minimum-edge threshold (Ziemba/Hausch suggested ≥10% to absorb estimation bias).
- **Closing line value (CLV).** The pre-off exchange price is the most efficient public estimate; consistently beating **Betfair SP** (BSP) is the fastest statistically-valid read on whether the model has edge — profitability follows sustained positive CLV with high probability ([Punter2Pro](https://punter2pro.com/punters-guide-beating-the-sp/), [StrideOdds on racing CLV](https://www.strideodds.ai/betting-logic/horse-racing-closing-line-value-how-to-measure-betting-edge)). BSP has no bookmaker margin (books' overrounds vs exchange ~100.5–101%), so backtests must use BSP-minus-commission (2–5%), never industry SP ([Betfair BSP](https://www.betfair.com.au/hub/education/betfair-basics/betfair-starting-price-bsp/), [geegeez SP vs BSP](https://www.geegeez.co.uk/comparing-starting-price-with-betfair-sp/)). Instrument the app to log model price vs BSP for every recommendation from day one.
- **Backtesting pitfalls specific to racing.**
  - *Post-race leakage:* SP itself, finishing-derived fields (RPR/Topspeed for that run, in-running comments, post-race OR revisions) must be lagged to their publication date. Ratings files as downloaded today embed revisions not known pre-race.
  - *Non-runners and Rule 4:* bookmaker odds carry Rule 4 deductions after withdrawals; late non-runners change the draw and pace map — backtests should either drop affected races or model deductions ([nonrunnerstomorrow on NR effects](https://nonrunnerstomorrow.com/draw-bias/)).
  - *Temporal discipline:* strictly walk-forward by racing season; purge/embargo around split boundaries; never k-fold-shuffle runners across time (horse/trainer identities leak) ([backtesting-pitfalls overviews](https://coriva.eu.org/en/backtesting-pitfalls/), [arXiv sports-betting review](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2410.21484)).
  - *Selection/survivorship:* testing ten angles and reporting the winner; using databases that silently drop abandoned meetings, voided races, or amateur/point-to-point form.
  - *Price realism:* assume you move the market (thin Irish midweek Betfair win markets may have £10k–£50k matched pre-off vs HK's $10M pools); Benter's 0.25–0.5%-of-turnover ceiling scales down brutally at small tracks — his explicit warning that "at small volume tracks one could probably not make enough money for the operation to be viable" applies to, e.g., Ballinrobe on a Tuesday.
- **Realistic edge and variance.** A strong operation converts a ΔR² of ~0.02 into single-digit-% ROI at high turnover; a losing season (Benter: −20%) is possible *with* a genuinely winning model. Any backtest showing +30% ROI over a few hundred bets is, with near-certainty, leakage or variance.

## 5. Open evidence: datasets, public models, verified records

- **Kaggle — Hong Kong:** [Horse Racing in HK (gdaley)](https://www.kaggle.com/datasets/gdaley/hkracing) — HKJC races and runs incl. sectional/position data, widely used in notebooks; [HK dataset 1997–2005 (~6,349 races)](https://github.com/constancedongg/Horse-Racing); [HK "for experts" dataset (~30,189 runner records, 19 variables)](https://www.kaggle.com/datasets/hrosebaby/horse-racing-dataset-for-experts-hong-kong); example modelling: [codeworks mvp-horse-racing-prediction](https://github.com/codeworks-data/mvp-horse-racing-prediction), [Cullen Sun deep model](https://www.kaggle.com/code/cullensun/deep-learning-model-for-hong-kong-horse-racing). No major *prize* Kaggle competition on racing was found — datasets and notebooks only (could not verify any official Kaggle horse-racing competition with leaderboard).

No public retail model with an independently *audited* long-term ROI in UK/Irish racing was found — could not verify any; tipster "proofed" records exist but survivorship-ridden. The credible verified records are the syndicates (Benter/Woods/Ranogajec) and the aggregate Premium Charge population.

## 6. Implications for the Ireland-first app

1. **Architecture:** conditional-logit-style softmax over each race (or GBDT with a listwise/Plackett–Luce loss), trained on lagged features only, then **Benter second-stage blend with exchange odds**; evaluate by ΔR²/log-loss vs odds-only and by walk-forward Kelly simulation at BSP−commission.
2. **Data floor:** Benter's 500–1,000-race minimum is easily met (Ireland runs ~2,000+ meetings-races/yr covered in the Kaggle/rpscrape data back to 1988), but full past-performance depth per runner is the real requirement — favour a database that includes UK form since Irish horses run in both jurisdictions constantly.
3. **Edge hypotheses ranked by literature support:** (a) sectional/pace-derived features in Ireland (market has only priced them since 2024); (b) handicap-progression features off OR; (c) shrunk trainer-form (cold-yard fades); (d) race-shape/pace projections; (e) draw×going×course microstructure — always odds-adjusted (A/E), since headline biases like Chester are over-bet.
4. **Expectations:** a good result is a few percent ROI at BSP after commission with heavy variance; validate via CLV within weeks rather than ROI over years; size with ≤½ Kelly and a minimum-edge + minimum-probability filter.
